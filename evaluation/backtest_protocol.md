# Sequential outcome survival

Liang and Lu (2026, Definition 3) ask whether an output remains generable after an input is removed, holding the generator fixed. Their implementation discussion also allows qualitative evidence about generability (pp. 14–15). We adapt that criterion to identifiable AI contributions within one research project.

Let C contain the declared research inputs and R the contributions removed. Ask whether a substantively equivalent, comparably supported outcome can be derived from C without R under the specified procedures. The target outcome is the object of evaluation, not an admissible premise. A matching sentence without an independent evidentiary argument does not establish survival.

## Retained inputs and procedures

State the available case evidence, eligible theory corpus, permitted reasoning or search procedures, and limits. Distinguish documented availability from reconstructed or assumed availability. Process logs are valuable for historical discovery attribution, but their absence does not prevent a retrospective test under explicit premises.

The main adaptation withdraws AI-provided inputs. Disabling the model itself changes the generative procedure and requires a separate specification. An AI-assisted evaluator may propose a derivation, but that proposal is not an observation of an unaided human discovering it.

## From Step 1 to Step 5

1. Identify the substantive outcome and proposed AI contribution at each step.
2. Withdraw the selected contribution and invalidate its dependent records, including human paraphrases and later AI outputs.
3. Seek an alternative derivation using eligible retained premises. It may be newly constructed after the target is known. Review the premises, inference, source support, and substantive equivalence.
4. Give an established replacement its own ID. Carry its actual claim and scope into the next step; do not restore the stronger original version silently.
5. Continue through the final reconstruction. An unresolved earlier outcome blocks routes that require it, but not an independently supported direct route.
6. Repeat for selected individual, joint, and cumulative removals. Section 5 of Liang and Lu motivates the groupwise check: an outcome can survive either single removal while depending on the contributions jointly.

The ledger includes eleven distinct removal specifications: five individual contributions, two adjacent pairs, all five together, and three additional cumulative prefixes. The Step 1 prefix and the five-step prefix coincide with existing removals. These specifications are within-project questions, not assignments to researchers.

## Evidence for survival

| Basis | What it establishes | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Previously documented independent derivation | A supported route was recorded without the contribution. | Support and independence still require review. |
| Retrospective evidentiary derivation | A supported route can now be demonstrated from retained premises. | Does not establish spontaneous discovery by the original researcher. |
| Bounded search or computational replay | An equivalent, weaker, or unrecovered outcome under specified procedures. | Finite non-recovery provides evidence, not proof of impossibility. |
| Dependency graph alone | Which old recorded routes are invalidated. | Cannot rule out new replacement routes. |
| Unsupported conjecture | A proposed possibility for further examination. | Does not establish equivalent supported recovery. |

Knowledge of the target is compatible with a retrospective derivation. The evaluator must show the argument rather than cite the removed suggestion as its own justification. The prospective Step 3 module still receives only its restricted literature-and-context packet; the complete evaluation record must not be used as that module’s input.

## Dependence and benefit

Report survival, substantive quality, and practical assistance separately. AI can accelerate search, improve source fidelity, or clarify an already attainable idea. Actual time saved or changes in the researcher’s judgment require corresponding records. An AI-dependent error is dependence without benefit. Removal may also improve the account.

Missing historical records constrain discovery attribution, not the eligibility of a retrospective survival test. Independence here concerns the premises and derivation, not a requirement that the evaluator has forgotten the target.

## Niu Lai boundary

The worked review retains the case note and the verified Berger, Sorensen, and Rasmussen (2010) source as its eligible corpus. All five qualified outcomes receive authored alternative derivations under removal of A1–A5. Step 5 survives as a research question and proposed test, not as an established mechanism. The source was curated for this illustration; independent discovery of it by the original researcher is not established. Withholding that source would require a separately specified backtest.
